How to Sell Your House Fast When a Job Relocation Takes You Out of Northern California

By Patrick Mahoney October 26, 2026 11 min read

You took the job. The start date is on the calendar, the movers are booked, and the house has to be sold before any of that happens. Or you have already left, and the house sits empty while you pay for it and a new place at the same time. Either way, a job relocation puts a hard deadline on a sale that normally runs on the market's schedule, not yours.

This post is for homeowners anywhere in Northern California who need to sell because of a move. It covers the timing squeeze, two housing payments, selling before or after you leave, renting instead, what relocation packages tend to cover, running a sale from out of state, what happens to a listing once the seller is gone, and how a cash sale with a closing date you pick fits in. For the full list of situations we work with, see situations we help.

What a relocation actually means for a sale

A normal listing waits on other people: an offer, then the buyer's lender, appraiser and inspector. If any of those slip, the closing date moves, and nothing in a standard purchase agreement cares about your first day of work. A relocation flips that. Your date is fixed and everything else has to bend around it.

The timing squeeze

Most people get a few weeks to a couple of months of notice. A financed sale generally needs a month or more from accepted offer to closing, and that clock does not start until you have a buyer. Add time to prep and show the house, and a conventional sale often runs past your first day at the new job. Selling before you go is cleaner: you are there for showings and signing, and you leave with the money, though you may take a lower price or heavier concessions to hit the date. Selling after you go removes the deadline but adds carrying costs and an empty house.

Carrying two housing payments

The real cost of a slow sale is the mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities, yard care and any HOA dues on the old house, stacked on top of rent or a new mortgage where you are going. Many insurance carriers also treat a vacant house differently after a month or two, and some will not keep a standard policy on an empty home at all. Ask your carrier before you leave.

Renting it out instead

Renting can feel like a way to avoid deciding. It works for some people, especially with a low payment and a plan to come back. Often it does not. You become a long-distance landlord on day one, and California tenant law is detailed enough that mistakes get expensive. Generally, the capital gains exclusion on a primary residence depends on having lived there two of the last five years, so a rental that drags on can cost you that break. Talk to a CPA first.

What relocation packages usually cover

Read the package closely before you count on it. Most cover the physical move and some temporary housing. Some reimburse part of the commissions or closing costs, and a few include an employer buyout through a relocation company, with its own appraisal, its own timeline and a price that is rarely generous. Few cover carrying costs on a house that does not sell, and almost none cover repair credits or price cuts you take to get it sold.

What happens to a listing once you are gone

An empty house shows differently. Buyers notice every scuff, and after a few weeks the question becomes what is wrong with it instead of what the price is. The usual answer is a price cut, then another. When an offer comes, the asks start: the inspection turns up items you cannot fix from another state, so you pay a credit; the appraisal comes in short; the buyer's financing wobbles a week before closing and the deal falls apart, and you start over with a listing that now shows as having fallen out of contract. Each step costs a month and a payment, managed by phone with a contractor you have never met.

Managing a sale from out of state

You do not have to fly back to close. Closing documents can be signed in front of a mobile notary who comes to you, or through remote online notarization where the title company accepts it. The deed generally needs a notarized signature, so plan on one notary appointment near your new home. A power of attorney can let someone local sign for you, but title companies have specific requirements, so have them approve the form first. Proceeds are wired to the account you choose.

Your options, honestly

  • List with an agent. If you have two or three months before your start date, the house is in good shape, and you can carry it if it does not sell quickly, listing is the better route and will usually net you more. Ask any agent how they run showings and signings once you are gone.
  • Sell to a cash buyer. If the date is close, the house needs work, or you cannot carry two payments, a cash buyer gives you a firm price and a closing date you choose. You trade some of the price for certainty and skip repairs, showings, commissions and the lender wait. Our guide to cash buyers versus agents in California walks through the tradeoff.
  • Take the employer buyout. If your package includes one, compare its number and timeline against a cash offer and a listing. It may be your best option, or just the floor.
  • Rent it out or leave it vacant. Workable if your payment is low and you have help nearby. Risky if you need the rent to cover the mortgage, or if the house will sit empty while you decide.

For a relocating seller we handle the visit on your schedule, the title company and lender payoff coordination, the notary appointment wherever you land, and anything left behind. Furniture or a garage full of boxes is fine.

What to have ready before you call

  • Your start date, and the date you need to be out of the house if it is different.
  • Your most recent mortgage statement, plus any second loan or HELOC, so the payoff can be estimated.
  • Your relocation package, if any, and what it says about home sale help.
  • Who else is on title and whether they can sign.
  • How we can get in once you are gone: a neighbor, a lockbox, or a time before you leave.
  • Any known issues with the house. Nothing disqualifies it, but knowing keeps the offer accurate.

How this works across Northern California

Escrow works the same everywhere in the state, but the shape of the move differs by county: a commuter who finally took a job near the office, a state worker or tech transfer, a second home that no longer fits, or a remote worker called back to an office.

We buy in all ten counties below. Each link goes to that county's page.

  • Sonoma County: the commuter belt along the 101 in Petaluma, Rohnert Park and Cotati is full of people whose jobs are in Marin or San Francisco, and a transfer out of that job, or out of Santa Rosa employers like Kaiser, Sutter, Keysight and Medtronic, is the move we hear about most.
  • Sacramento County: state workers, UC Davis Health staff and Intel employees in Folsom make up a steady share of moves, and Bay Area relocations run in both directions.
  • Contra Costa County: sellers in Walnut Creek, Concord, Antioch and Richmond are often commuters whose job moved and who no longer want the BART or Highway 4 drive.
  • Napa County: in Napa, St. Helena and American Canyon we see second homes and wine-industry transfers where the owner already lives elsewhere.
  • Mendocino County: we buy in Ukiah, Fort Bragg and Willits, where the nearest title office can be a long drive and remote signing matters more than usual.
  • Marin County: San Rafael, Novato and Mill Valley sellers are often leaving a San Francisco job or letting go of a second home.
  • Solano County: Fairfield, Vacaville and Vallejo see steady military moves tied to Travis Air Force Base, and orders rarely wait for a listing to sell.
  • Placer County: tech transfers in and out of Roseville and Rocklin are the usual story, and we also buy in Lincoln and Auburn.
  • El Dorado County: remote workers who moved to South Lake Tahoe, El Dorado Hills, Cameron Park or Placerville and are now called back to an office are a common call.
  • Nevada County: Truckee, Grass Valley and Nevada City have the same return-to-office pattern, with the added problem of winter access for showings in an empty house.

When you need a professional first

Call a CPA before you decide between selling and renting, especially if you have lived in the house less than two years, because the primary residence exclusion and depreciation rules change what you keep. Talk to a real estate attorney if you are selling under a power of attorney, if a co-owner is not cooperating, or if the house is in a trust or an estate. If the relocation came alongside a divorce or a death in the family, see our guides on selling a house during a divorce in California and selling an inherited house in California, because each adds its own rules. None of this stops a sale. It just keeps it from costing more than it should.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I sell my house before I move for the new job or after?

Before is cleaner if you have the time. You are there for showings and signing, and you leave with the money. If your start date is close, selling after you move is common, but you carry two housing payments and an empty house in the meantime. A cash buyer who closes on your chosen date lets you sell before you go even on short notice.

Can I sell my California house from out of state without flying back?

Yes, in most cases. The title company can send closing documents to you for signing in front of a mobile notary near your new home, or use remote online notarization where accepted. Proceeds are wired to your bank. A power of attorney can also work, but have the title company approve the form first, since their requirements are specific.

Will my relocation package pay for selling my house?

It depends on the package. Many cover the move itself and temporary housing. Some reimburse part of the commissions or closing costs, and a few offer an employer buyout through a relocation company. Few cover the mortgage on a house that does not sell, and almost none cover repair credits or price cuts. Read the policy before you decide how to sell.

Should I rent the house out instead of selling before I relocate?

It can work if your payment is low, you have reliable local help, and you may come back. It often does not, because long-distance landlording in California is demanding and a rental that runs several years can cost you the capital gains exclusion on a primary residence. Talk to a CPA about the tax side before you decide.

How fast can a cash buyer close if my start date is two weeks away?

We can close in as few as 7 days, and most of our closings land in 7 to 14 days. You get a written offer within 24 hours of our visit, then escrow opens with a local title company and you pick the date. If you need a few days in the house after closing to finish packing, tell us up front and we will try to work it into the agreement.
