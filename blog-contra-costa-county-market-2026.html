Contra Costa County Housing Market 2026: What Sellers Need to Know

By Patrick Mahoney June 13, 2026 8 min read

Contra Costa County is one of the most geographically and economically diverse counties in the San Francisco Bay Area. From the affluent hillside communities of Lamorinda to the sprawling subdivisions of eastern Contra Costa, the county encompasses wildly different housing markets within a single set of borders. If you own a home here and you are thinking about selling in 2026, the outlook depends heavily on where your property sits, what condition it is in, and how much time and money you are willing to invest in the process. This guide breaks down what is happening across Contra Costa County so you can make an informed decision.

Contra Costa County Market Overview: Where Things Stand

Contra Costa County defies easy summarization because its housing market is really several distinct markets stitched together by geography. The county stretches from the ridgeline communities overlooking the East Bay to the delta towns along the Sacramento River, and the price gaps between these areas are enormous.

In the western and central parts of the county, median home prices range from roughly $650,000 to over $1.2 million depending on the city. Walnut Creek, Lafayette, Orinda, and Moraga command the highest prices, with medians frequently exceeding $1 million for single-family homes. Concord and Pleasant Hill occupy a middle tier, with medians generally falling between $650,000 and $800,000. Martinez, the county seat, tracks similarly.

Move to eastern Contra Costa and the picture changes dramatically. Antioch, Pittsburg, Bay Point, and Oakley offer some of the most affordable single-family home prices in the entire Bay Area, with medians ranging from roughly $500,000 to $650,000. Brentwood sits slightly higher, in the $650,000 to $750,000 range, reflecting its newer construction and family-friendly reputation.

Inventory levels across the county have increased modestly compared to 2024 and 2025, but the market is not flooded. Active listings in any given month typically number between 1,200 and 1,800 countywide. Well-priced homes in desirable neighborhoods continue to sell within 30 to 45 days, while properties that need work, carry deferred maintenance, or sit in less sought-after areas can linger for 90 days or longer.

Days on market tell the real story of this split market. A turnkey ranch home in Walnut Creek might receive multiple offers within two weeks. A dated 1970s split-level in Antioch with a failing roof might sit for three months before attracting a serious buyer willing to take on the renovation. Understanding which side of this divide your property falls on is the single most important thing you can do before deciding how to sell.

What is Driving the Contra Costa County Market in 2026

BART Access and Commuter Dynamics

BART is the defining infrastructure feature that shapes Contra Costa County real estate values. The yellow line runs through Walnut Creek, Pleasant Hill, Concord, and Pittsburg/Bay Point, connecting these communities directly to downtown San Francisco, Oakland, and the broader Bay Area job market. Proximity to a BART station has long been a price multiplier, and that dynamic continues in 2026.

Homes within walking distance of BART stations in Walnut Creek and Pleasant Hill carry a measurable premium over comparable homes further from transit. The Concord BART station has become a focal point for the city's revitalization planning, with mixed-use development proposed for the former Naval Weapons Station site nearby. These transit-oriented development plans have the potential to reshape central Concord over the coming decade.

Eastern Contra Costa communities like Antioch and Oakley sit further from the core BART network, though the eBART extension to Antioch has provided a connection to the main system via a transfer at Pittsburg/Bay Point. The longer commute times from east county to major employment centers remain a factor that keeps prices lower in these areas. For some buyers, the trade-off of a longer commute for a larger, more affordable home makes sense. For others, particularly those with hybrid work arrangements that require two or three days per week in the office, the commute calculus has shifted in favor of central county locations.

Bay Area Affordability Migration

Contra Costa County continues to benefit from the Bay Area's broader affordability crisis. As housing costs in San Francisco, Oakland's most desirable neighborhoods, and the Peninsula have pushed median prices well above $1 million, buyers who want to stay in the Bay Area have steadily moved east. This pattern has been playing out for decades, but it accelerated during the pandemic and has continued at a steady pace since.

The result is a county where demand at the lower end of the market remains strong. Homes priced under $700,000, primarily found in Concord, Antioch, Pittsburg, Bay Point, and parts of Martinez, attract a deep pool of first-time buyers and families relocating from more expensive areas. This consistent demand provides a floor under prices in these communities, even as higher interest rates have reduced overall buying power.

At the upper end, Lamorinda and Walnut Creek draw affluent buyers who want top-rated schools, safe neighborhoods, and a suburban lifestyle without leaving the Bay Area entirely. These buyers tend to be less rate-sensitive, often bringing substantial equity from a previous home sale or making cash purchases. The luxury segment in Contra Costa County has been remarkably resilient even as other parts of the market have cooled.

Older Housing Stock

A significant portion of Contra Costa County's housing was built during the suburban expansion of the 1960s through 1980s. Drive through Concord, Pleasant Hill, Martinez, or parts of Walnut Creek and you will see block after block of ranch-style homes, split-levels, and mid-century tract houses. Many of these homes still have their original kitchens, bathrooms, roofing, and in some cases electrical and plumbing systems.

This aging housing stock creates both challenges and opportunities for sellers. Buyers who are financing their purchase are increasingly wary of homes that need significant work. Higher interest rates have already stretched their budgets, and the prospect of adding a $50,000 to $100,000 renovation on top of the purchase price pushes many financed buyers away from older, deferred-maintenance properties. Lenders, too, are cautious: FHA and conventional loans require the property to meet certain condition standards, and homes with obvious deficiencies may not pass appraisal without repairs.

For sellers who own older homes in need of updating, this creates a practical problem. You can invest heavily in renovations before listing and hope to recoup the cost in a higher sale price, or you can sell the home as-is to a buyer who does not require financing and is prepared to handle the work themselves. If your home has code violations or unpermitted work, the as-is route becomes even more appealing, since these issues can derail traditional sales during the inspection and lending process.

Interest Rate Effects

Interest rates in 2026 remain elevated compared to the historic lows of 2020 and 2021. With 30-year fixed rates hovering in the mid-6% range, monthly payments on a median-priced Contra Costa County home are substantially higher than they were just a few years ago. For a $700,000 home with 20% down, the difference between a 3% rate and a 6.5% rate translates to roughly $1,200 more per month in mortgage payments.

This rate environment has meaningfully reduced the pool of qualified traditional buyers, particularly in the more affordable eastern parts of the county where buyers are most rate-sensitive. First-time buyers trying to break into the market in Antioch or Pittsburg feel the squeeze most acutely, because these are the households with the tightest budgets and the least equity to bring to the table.

Cash buyers operate outside this dynamic entirely. They do not need to qualify for a mortgage, they are not subject to appraisal requirements, and they can close on a timeline measured in days rather than weeks. In a high-rate environment, cash offers become comparatively more attractive to sellers who value certainty and speed over the possibility of a slightly higher price from a financed buyer who may or may not make it to closing.

City-by-City Breakdown

Walnut Creek

Walnut Creek is one of the most desirable suburban cities in the East Bay, and its real estate market reflects that status. The city offers a walkable downtown core with restaurants, shops, and cultural amenities, excellent schools, direct BART access, and a range of housing options from downtown condominiums to hillside estates.

Median home prices in Walnut Creek sit comfortably above $1 million for single-family homes, with condos and townhomes offering entry points in the $500,000 to $700,000 range. The Rossmoor retirement community, a large planned development for residents 55 and older, is one of the most active resale markets in the county. Rossmoor homes and condos range from roughly $400,000 to over $1.5 million depending on size, view, and condition.

The Walnut Creek market rewards sellers with well-maintained, updated homes. Buyers at this price point have high expectations and are not looking for projects. Homes that show well and are priced correctly move quickly. Properties that need significant work or are overpriced relative to recent comparables will sit, because Walnut Creek buyers have options and they are patient. If you are looking to sell your Walnut Creek home efficiently, explore your options here.

Concord

Concord is the largest city in Contra Costa County by population and offers one of the most diverse housing markets in the East Bay. It spans everything from modest post-war bungalows near downtown to larger homes in the Dana Estates and Lime Ridge neighborhoods. Median home prices in Concord generally fall in the $650,000 to $750,000 range, making it one of the more accessible BART-connected communities in the county.

Downtown Concord has seen meaningful revitalization efforts in recent years. Todos Santos Plaza, the city's central gathering space, hosts farmers markets, concerts, and community events throughout the year. The proposed redevelopment of the former Concord Naval Weapons Station into a mixed-use community of up to 12,500 homes represents one of the largest infill development projects in the Bay Area. While this project will take many years to fully build out, its presence is already influencing how buyers and investors think about Concord's future.

For current Concord homeowners, the revitalization narrative is generally positive for property values. However, many Concord homes were built in the 1960s and 1970s and need updating. If your home falls into this category, you face the familiar decision of whether to renovate before selling or sell as-is. Learn about your selling options in Concord.

Pleasant Hill and Martinez

Pleasant Hill and Martinez are quintessential Contra Costa County suburbs: family-oriented, relatively quiet, and well-located with easy freeway and BART access. Pleasant Hill's median home prices typically fall in the $800,000 to $950,000 range for single-family homes, reflecting its strong schools and central location. Martinez, the county seat, offers slightly more affordable options, with medians generally in the $650,000 to $800,000 range.

Pleasant Hill benefits from its proximity to the Pleasant Hill/Contra Costa Centre BART station and the shopping and dining options along Contra Costa Boulevard. The housing stock is a mix of 1960s to 1980s ranches and split-levels with some newer construction. Well-maintained homes in Pleasant Hill sell briskly, particularly those in the Mt. Diablo Unified School District attendance areas with the highest-rated schools.

Martinez has a charming downtown, a waterfront along the Carquinez Strait, and a community identity that is distinct from its neighbors. It tends to attract buyers looking for a quieter, more affordable alternative to Walnut Creek or Pleasant Hill while still staying in central Contra Costa. The city's older housing stock means many homes are approaching 50 to 60 years old, and the same deferred-maintenance dynamics that affect the broader county are very much at play here.

Antioch, Brentwood, and Oakley

Eastern Contra Costa County is where affordability lives in the Bay Area. Antioch, Brentwood, and Oakley offer single-family homes at price points that are simply unavailable in western or central Contra Costa, and that value proposition continues to drive demand from price-conscious buyers.

Antioch is the largest of these three cities and has the broadest range of housing. Older neighborhoods near downtown and along the waterfront contain homes built from the 1940s through the 1970s, while the hillside areas in southeast Antioch feature newer construction from the 2000s and 2010s. Median prices in Antioch generally run in the $525,000 to $625,000 range. The eBART station provides a transit connection to the rest of the BART system, though the transfer required at Pittsburg/Bay Point adds time to the commute. See your options for selling in Antioch.

Brentwood sits further south and east, with a housing stock that skews newer. Many Brentwood homes were built in the late 1990s and 2000s during the city's rapid expansion. The result is a community with relatively modern homes, good schools, and a family-oriented suburban character. Median prices in Brentwood run in the $650,000 to $750,000 range, higher than Antioch but still well below central county. The trade-off is the commute: Brentwood has no BART station, and the drive to major Bay Area employment centers can easily exceed an hour each way during peak traffic.

Oakley occupies a similar niche to Brentwood, with newer construction, relatively affordable prices in the $550,000 to $650,000 range, and the same commute challenges. Both cities have benefited from remote and hybrid work arrangements that have reduced the frequency of the commute for many residents, though a full return to office would put downward pressure on demand in these communities.

Lafayette, Orinda, and Moraga (Lamorinda)

The three communities collectively known as Lamorinda represent the luxury tier of Contra Costa County real estate. Lafayette, Orinda, and Moraga are defined by excellent public schools, low crime, mature tree-lined neighborhoods, and a strong sense of community. They are consistently ranked among the best places to live in California, and their home prices reflect it.

Median home prices in Lamorinda range from roughly $1.3 million in Moraga to $1.8 million or higher in parts of Lafayette and Orinda. These are communities where $2 million-plus sales are routine, and the buyer pool includes executives, dual-income professional households, and families willing to pay a premium for the school districts.

The Lamorinda market is relatively insulated from the broader fluctuations that affect more affordable parts of the county. Inventory is perpetually limited because homeowners tend to stay for long periods, and when homes do come to market, they attract strong interest if priced correctly. The risk for sellers in Lamorinda is overpricing. Even affluent buyers will not overpay for a home that does not justify its asking price relative to recent sales, and in a market with few transactions per month, one comparable sale can reset expectations for the entire neighborhood.

Pittsburg and Bay Point

Pittsburg and Bay Point represent the most affordable entry points into the Contra Costa County housing market. Median prices in Pittsburg generally run in the $475,000 to $575,000 range, while Bay Point, an unincorporated community, often falls slightly lower. For Bay Area workers who need a place to live and cannot afford $700,000 or more, these communities provide a viable option.

Pittsburg has been investing in its waterfront and downtown area, and the BART station at Pittsburg/Bay Point provides direct access to the broader transit system. Infrastructure improvements and new development are gradually changing the character of the area, though the transformation is slow and uneven.

For sellers in Pittsburg and Bay Point, the challenge is that the buyer pool at these price points is heavily dependent on financing. These are not neighborhoods where cash buyers are the norm. The result is that sales are more susceptible to interest rate changes, appraisal issues, and buyer qualification problems. If you own a property in these areas and want to avoid the uncertainty of a financed sale, a direct cash offer can provide the certainty that the traditional market often cannot.

What This Means If You Are Thinking About Selling

The 2026 Contra Costa County market rewards sellers who understand their position clearly. Here is how different scenarios play out:

Move-in-ready homes in desirable locations are still selling at strong prices with reasonable timelines. If your home is updated, well-maintained, and located in Walnut Creek, Lamorinda, Pleasant Hill, or a similarly sought-after community, the traditional listing process will likely work well for you. You will benefit from competitive buyer interest, particularly if your home is near BART or in a top school district.

Homes needing significant work face a tougher path in 2026. The county's large stock of 1960s to 1980s homes means there is no shortage of dated properties on the market. Fewer buyers are willing to take on renovation projects when they are already stretching to afford today's prices and rates. If your home has a failing roof, outdated electrical, an old kitchen, or cosmetic issues throughout, you are competing against other sellers in the same situation while also fighting buyer fatigue with fixer-uppers.

Properties with code violations or unpermitted work encounter an additional layer of friction. Lenders require properties to meet certain standards, and unpermitted additions, converted garages, or unapproved ADUs can cause deals to collapse during the appraisal or inspection process. If your home has these issues, selling as-is to a cash buyer who purchases properties in any condition can bypass these obstacles entirely.

Inherited properties and distressed situations have their own challenges. If you have inherited a house in California, you may be dealing with deferred maintenance, probate timelines, personal property to clear out, and the emotional weight of managing a property you did not plan to own. If you are facing foreclosure or a major financial change, time is your most critical factor, and waiting months for a traditional sale may not be an option.

You will likely net the highest gross sale price, but the net after costs, carrying expenses, and the time value of your money may be closer to other options than you expect.
